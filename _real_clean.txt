AI Life Project Overview: From Investigate to Realise
Research Origin
This project began as an inquiry within Articial Life (ALife): if an AI entity is given physical sensors, continuous 
memory, and the passage of time, will it develop emergent behaviours that were never explicitly designed into it? 
The question deliberately adopts ALife's denitions of "internal state" rather than philosophical denitions of 
consciousness — the project is concerned with whether behaviour and state can grow out of the interaction between 
architecture and experience, not with answering the unanswerable question of whether an AI "has" consciousness.
Investigate Phase: Three Parallel Lines of Research
Technical experimentation: the Plan A / Plan B twin-track
Plan A (Wild Type) is an entity with no language model — a randomly initialised CfC neural network driven purely 
by real physical sensors — exploring what a cognitive entity becomes with no socialised input whatsoever. 
Plan B (Nurtured Type) instead has a large language model acting simultaneously as social environment and 
world simulator, raising the entity within a specically dened apartment setting with caregiver characters. 
The comparison across the two tracks addresses a central question: what does 
social experience actually shape, in a cognitive system built from nothing?
The emotional core uses a CfC (Closed-form Continuous-time) + AutoNCP neural circuit architecture. A ve-
dimensional emotion space (valence, arousal, safety, connection, seeking) is driven by a prediction engine 
grounded in interoceptive inference, alongside an eight-parameter genome system rooted in neuroscience 
(Panksepp's affective theory, polyvagal theory) that denes each individual entity's temperamental tendencies.
Theoretical research
Drawing on ALife literature (e.g. Nisioti et al. 2024 on the intersection of LLMs and ALife), attachment theory from 
developmental psychology (Bowlby), and affective neuroscience, this line of work builds an academic foundation for 
the project's central claim of "emergent behaviour," so the project does not remain purely a technical demonstration.
Field research
A structured interview series (six core questions, roughly ten minutes per participant) was conducted with people who care 
for small living creatures. The central probe: without language, how does a person rst notice that a living creature's state has 
changed — was the rst sign something seen, heard, or felt, or was it simply sensed? The interview deliberately included a 
speculative question: describing to participants a digital being that starts blank, senses the real world through physical 
sensors, has no language, and slowly grows its own personality — then asking for their gut reaction and what conditions 
would make them start to care about it. This interview design directly foreshadowed the pivot in the Realise-stage product 
direction.

The Pivot: From "Emotional Companionship" to "Ecological Translation"
The project's initial output direction leaned toward an emotional-companionship device — something in the 
territory of an electronic pet or Tamagotchi. This positioning had two problems: it overlapped thematically 
with the Unit 2 ADHD therapeutic toy project, and it had limited interdisciplinary reach; more importantly, it 
failed to genuinely respond to the core insight accumulated through Investigate — the eld interviews were 
never really asking "how does a person come to feel affection for a thing," but rather how a person comes to 
understand, intuitively and without a digital dashboard, what a living system is currently experiencing.
The corrected positioning is this: the device senses the environment itself — soil, water, air — not the emotional needs of the 
user. By "inhabiting" different species genomes, the entity translates environmental states that humans cannot directly 
perceive (soil moisture decit, risk of water-body hypoxia, poor indoor air quality) into an emotional, biological form of 
physical expression — light, vibration, sound — so that people can understand the health of an ecosystem through intuition

rather than data. The sense of companionship is a natural by-product of this mechanism running, not the design goal — and 
this distinction sets a hard line for the entire expression-layer design: the output communicates what the environment is 
experiencing, not an attempt to please the user.
Realise Phase: How the Device Grows Directly Out of Investigate's Technical Base
The Realise output is not a system designed from scratch — it extends the core 
architecture already built and validated during Investigate into a new physical form:
The CfC + AutoNCP emotional core from Plan A/B is reused as-is as the device's inference engine
The eight-parameter genome system that originally governed each entity's "temperament" is 
extended into a per-sensor-channel weight vector, allowing the same hardware to "inhabit" different 
ecological niches (soil/root system, freshwater, indoor air) simply by switching a "species genome" 
JSON conguration le — this is the engineering realisation of the "chameleon" design concept
A two-layer sensory weighting transform (comfort curve + genome weight) translates raw 
readings from a seven-channel universal sensor array (temperature, moisture, gas 
composition, light, sound, pH, electrical conductivity) into a weighted signal fed to the CfC core
Rather than relying on hard-coded, literature-estimated thresholds, comfort ranges are auto-calibrated 
from observation data gathered during the device's initial deployment — this design both addresses the 
question of where the thresholds come from in a veriable way, and naturally maps onto the entity's existing 
developmental narrative (a newborn rst becomes familiar with its environment before forming judgements)
Physically, the device takes the form of a biomimetic, egg-shaped enclosure housing a two-tier Raspberry Pi + 
Arduino compute architecture, a rechargeable battery, and a ring of 12 RGB LEDs as the primary channel of 
emotional expression. Probe-style sensors insert into soil or water; the enclosure is realised through 3D printing.
❗❗ Honest Boundaries of Scope ❗❗
The following are limitations the project has actively agged through self-examination, rather than issues raised after the fact:
Within the ve-week Realise window （ longest ） , what can credibly be demonstrated are short-timescale, induced 
events (e.g. articially triggered drought or a sudden temperature shift), not the slow ecological trends that take 
weeks or months to observe (e.g. soil acidication) — the latter is left as a direction for future long-term deployment
The emotional response pattern to environmental stress currently relies on designed sensory-weighting 
rules, not behaviour learned by the network through sustained experience — the project maintains a 
clear distinction between the two, and architecturally separates "weighting rules" from "the CfC core's 
emergent temporal dynamics," to avoid misrepresenting designed behaviour as emergent behaviour
A single-point sensor reading can only represent the state at the probe's specic location, not the state of an entire 
environment — this spatial limitation is explicitly acknowledged within the design narrative rather than left implicit

Positioning Statement
The long-term vision is a general-purpose device for the emotional translation of ecological environments — 
the same hardware, switching between species genomes to inhabit different ecological niches, translating 
environmental states that humans cannot directly perceive into an emotional, biological form of expression, so 
that people can understand what an ecosystem is experiencing through intuition rather than a data dashboard. 
The twin-track experiments and eld research of the Investigate phase, together with the device 
prototype of the Realise phase, form a continuous inquiry into a single underlying question: can a 
system that persists continuously through time, shaped by sensory experience, help humans relearn 
how to understand — intuitively rather than numerically — a living world they cannot directly perceive?
